MISSION BRIEFING

How to Survive on Mars

🎯 YOUR GOAL

Run Jezero Base for 20 sols (a sol is one Mars day). Collect 80 Science and keep the crew alive until the end of Sol 20 to win. The base wears down faster every sol, so reaching the science goal early does not win by itself. You lose if O₂, Shield or Food reaches 0%, or the crew's radiation dose reaches 100%.

Everything you do costs power (kW), and you only get a limited amount each sol. So the real game is choosing what to power and what to leave off.

🔁 HOW A SOL (DAY) WORKS
  1. Watch for weather days. Solar flares and dust storms fill a whole day. Some warn you the day before, but not all: use Weather Outlook at the Comms Dish to look ahead, then prepare on the day before.
  2. Click a building to open its control panel on the right, and press its actions there. Or press V for first person, walk to a building's door and press E to go inside and press the glowing wall buttons. Each action can be used once per sol.
  3. Collect scrap and explore if you like (see below).
  4. There are no chores: every action costs power or scrap and only improves your chances. Spend your power on what matters most, then press End Sol & Decide. First comes the evening decision: one big choice with three plans (safe, balanced or bold). Then the night passes: supplies drain, new power arrives and the next day begins.
📊 WHAT EACH NUMBER MEANS
O₂ (oxygen)Air for the crew. Drops about 7% a sol at first and MORE every sol as the base wears down (a Habitat upgrade slows it). At 0% the mission ends. Make more with MOXIE in the Habitat.
SHIELD (radiation shielding)How well the walls block space radiation. Drops about 3% a sol (5% on weather days), rising as the base wears down. The lower it is, the faster the dose climbs and the harder flares hit. At 0% the mission ends. Reinforce it in the Habitat.
FOODCrew meals. Drops about 6% a sol at first and more every sol (a Silo upgrade slows it). At 0% the mission ends. Harvest crops in the Food Silo.
DOSE (radiation)Radiation the crew has absorbed. It only goes UP (lower is better). About +1 or +2% every sol (more when shielding is low), plus a big jump on solar-flare days unless you prepare. At 100% the crew must go home. Above 60% dose, or with crew morale under 30, your science results are halved.
POWER (kW)Your budget for today. It refills each sol: about 6–10 kW when panels are open and clean, only 5 when they are stowed or in a dust storm, minus standby costs.
BATT (battery)Stored power. Charge it on a good day (costs 3 kW for +2) and use it on a bad day.
EVENING DECISIONEvery sol ends with one choice. Read the situation, check the numbers, and pick a plan. Four plans, and none is labelled. Read what each one gives and costs, and watch for “Could go either way” plans. Keep 2–3 kW in reserve so you can afford more of them. Your choices carry into the night.
NEXT SOLAn estimate of how much power you will get tomorrow after modules that are ON use their standby power.
SCIENCEYour score. You need the goal AND a crew still alive at the end of Sol 20. Earn it with the spectrometer, comms uplinks, the rover and exploring.
SCRAPSalvaged metal. Pay for upgrades and repairs (module upgrades, the rover, shielding, patching leaks).
UPGRADES (Lv 1 to 4)Every module can be upgraded three times. Each level costs more scrap (2, then 4, then 7) but gives a bigger bonus: stronger actions, free gains each sol, slower wear. The Comms Array also lets Weather Outlook see 1, 2, then 3 sols ahead.
MODULE POWERSwitching a module OFF saves its standby kW every sol, but that building wears down twice as fast and its buttons lock. Habitat and Silo also leak air, shield and food. Good for a dark day, bad for a long time.
Module health barsEach building has a hidden condition (crew morale, panel cleanliness, instrument calibration, crop health, antenna alignment). They fade each sol. Under 70% that module only gives 75% of its results, under 40% only half. Crew morale also leaks air and shield. Calibration sets Lab science, alignment sets Comms science, how far the Outlook sees, how many warnings you get and how fast Earth replies. Panel cleanliness is up to half your power. Crop health and water set your harvest size. Maintenance buttons restore them.
🏠 THE FIVE MODULES + ON/OFF SWITCHES

🏠 Habitat: oxygen, shielding, storm shelter, movie night. · ☀️ Solar Array: power, battery, dust brushing. · 🔬 Research Lab: science and calibration. · 🌱 Food Silo: crops and spare parts. · 📡 Comms Dish: send science to Earth.

Every module except solar has a POWER switch on the left side of its door. ON uses standby power each sol (Habitat 1, Lab 2, Silo 1, Comms 1 kW) but gives small free gains by itself. OFF saves that power, but locks the module's buttons (except the storm shelter), makes its health bar fade twice as fast, and the Habitat and Silo leak supplies. Floating labels over each building show its ON/OFF state.

🔩 SCRAP, HILLS & THE ROVER

Raw scrap must be processed. Scrap you pick up outside or get from the rover is RAW (see the RAW counter at the top). Press PROCESS RAW SCRAP in the Food Silo workshop (1 kW, up to 3 pieces at a time) to turn it into usable scrap for upgrades and repairs.

Each sol, 1–3 pieces of scrap appear as glowing amber markers on the map. Click one to claim it (in first person, aim at it within about 9 m). The minimap shows them as yellow squares.

The cyan beacons on the far hills are exploration sites (cyan diamonds on the minimap). Click one to send the rover (2 kW). It drives there and comes back after 2–4 sols with science. The first visit pays the most. Or walk there in first person and survey on foot for +2 science (adds a little radiation).

⚠️ FORECAST & SURPRISES

Weather days: a solar flare or a dust storm fills a whole day. Some warn you a sol ahead on their own; the Comms Weather Outlook finds the rest. Prepare on the day before, because the damage lands at dawn (dust) or during the day (flare).

Every building has a prep button: Habitat Prepare Storm Shelter (flare) or Scrub Air Filters (dust), Solar Stow Panels, Lab Secure Instruments, Silo Cover Crops, Comms Stow Dish. Each costs little power. A building you did not prepare is damaged: instrument calibration, crop health and dish alignment drop sharply, panels get scratched or dirty, and an unsheltered crew takes a big radiation dose.

Evening decisions: every sol ends with one choice between three plans (safe, balanced or bold). Bold plans can pay off or backfire.

Data sources: archived solar flare dates from NASA DONKI (not live); radiation from Curiosity RAD; dust storms, ice, soil and relay facts from NASA Phoenix, Opportunity, InSight, Ingenuity and Perseverance mission archives.
